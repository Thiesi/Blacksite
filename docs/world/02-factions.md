# Blacksite — Faction Dossiers

Expands `docs/world/00-bible.md` §7. The mechanical rules for standing,
membership, relations, Marked, and relays are in
`docs/design/00-game-design.md` §7 and are not restated here except where
a faction bends them by flavour. Where this document and the bible
disagree, the bible wins.

Zone slugs below are the ones the gazetteer (`01-gazetteer.md`) is
expected to use. If the gazetteer names a hall differently, the gazetteer
is the truth for slugs and this document must be corrected.

Colour fallbacks are given as truecolor hex, then the xterm-256 index,
then the 16-colour ANSI index. In 16-colour mode several factions share a
hue; that is acceptable because no faction is ever identified by colour
alone (the sigil glyph and the label always accompany it).

---

## How a faction is presented to a player

Every faction has the same five surfaces in the game:

1. **The recruiter's card** in the Wake (the pitch below, verbatim).
2. **The hall**: one zone with a handler, a vendor, a vat (where the
   faction has one), a contract board, and a Descent console.
3. **Ranks**: cosmetic titles at standing +30, +50, +70, +90, shown after
   the character name in sitrep and on target. Rank names are the
   faction's own vocabulary; a Ferryman at +90 is not a "Captain".
4. **Contracts**: generated from templates in the faction's voice, plus
   one story contract per season.
5. **Chat**: the faction channel, which has its own culture (section at
   the end).

## 1. Halvard-Ostrom Dynamics

*"Containment is a kindness."*

**Colours:** gunmetal `#6B7280` (256: 243, 16: 8) on amber `#F59E0B`
(256: 214, 16: 3). **Sigil:** `⊞` (ASCII `#`). **Visual tell:** matte
grey plate, amber visor strip, everything squared off. Halvard people
stand like furniture.

### History since the Severance

Halvard's engineers were the ones who physically cut Karst's uplinks the
night of the Severance, and they have never let anyone forget it. That
act saved the Lattice and, in Halvard's telling, the city. In everyone
else's telling it also meant Halvard was the only organisation in Karst
with a complete picture of what was happening for the first nine hours,
and chose to spend them sealing the shaft under the Spire.

The company spent the Long Quiet turning inward. It pulled its security
forces off the streets, ceded civil order to the Civic Authority, and
poured concrete. The lower Spire became a fortress; the upper Spire
became the only place in Karst where the lights never flickered. Halvard
kept its subsidiaries close (Kestrel is still, on paper, a Halvard
company, and Sablier's original concession was a Halvard contract) and
let the rest of the city fend for itself.

When the Choir started hearing things, Halvard declared it a public-
health matter and offered Sablier a bounty per resonance implant
removed. Sablier declined, politely, and kept selling them. That was the
end of Halvard's pretence that its allies were its subordinates.

Anselm Halvard, the founder's grandson, took the directorship in Year 33
after a boardroom quarrel that ended with two vice-directors decanted
from backup and a third not decanted at all. He has spent eight years
building the case that the Exhale is a breach, not a mystery, and that
the only sane response is more concrete.

Halvard is not the villain. Halvard is the faction that has read the
most of the Site Zero fragments and is the most frightened.

### Structure and ranks

Halvard is a corporation with a security arm, and members are contract
security first. Ranks by standing: **Contractor** (+30), **Retainer**
(+50), **Warrant** (+70), **Custodial Officer** (+90). "Custodial" is
the company's own word and it does not like being asked about it.

### Leadership

- **Director Anselm Halvard** (leader). Fifty-one, tall, exact, wears a
  suit the colour of a filing cabinet. He is not cruel; he is convinced,
  which is worse. He reads the Silt reports personally. He has never
  been below the Spire's public levels and says so as if it were a
  virtue. *"I am the only person in this city who has read the sealing
  order. You will forgive me if I do not take a vote."*
- **Commander Ruth Ashgrove**, head of Spire Security. Ex-Warden, left
  the Civic Authority after a curfew that went wrong and never explained
  which one. Runs Halvard's patrols in the Scour and takes the losses
  personally. *"Patrol Three is late. Patrol Three is never late. Get me
  a runner."*
- **Dr. Emeric Lassiter**, Archivist. The man who holds the fragments.
  Old, small, delighted by everything, and the most dangerous person in
  the Spire because he has stopped believing the sealing order was
  right. *"Do you know what the word 'custodian' meant originally? A
  janitor. Isn't that lovely."*
- **Petra Vael**, Recruiter. Wake Hall liaison, brisk, good at her job,
  visibly counting the days until her own Ninety-Day anniversary. She
  was a Wake too.

**Hall:** `spire-atrium` (the Spire's public levels). Halvard's
vat is on the atrium level and is the cleanest in the city.

### Recruiter's pitch (Petra Vael)

> You were decanted from one of our templates, did you know that? Most
> of the Wake Hall was. Halvard-Ostrom built this city, cut the cables
> that saved it, and has kept the shaft sealed for forty-one years while
> everyone else argued about what to do. We are not popular. We are not
> trying to be. We are hiring people who understand that the thing under
> our feet is not a mystery, it is a hazard, and that hazards get
> managed. Good gear, good clinic, the Spire's view. Take the card.

### Vendor

Kinetic weapons (Halvard's own subsidiaries), heavy armour plate, Spire-
grade medical consumables, patrol drones for Operators at high standing,
Scour survival kit, and, at Warrant and above, the only legal source of
**containment charges** (the item that cuts a core's power from outside
its room).

### Contracts

Templates: *Patrol* (clear an outer Scour ring of Ferrymen scavengers,
never phrased as Ferrymen), *Recovery* (fetch a specific Ring-fall object
before anyone else does), *Seal* (plant a Halvard beacon in an Undercity
core that has started transmitting).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — Patrol Three's last runner is found in the
Undercity, jacked in and alive, and Halvard wants her back before the
Choir gets to her. **Season 2** — Lassiter quietly asks a trusted crew
to *not* deliver a fragment they recover, and to tell no one, including
him. **Season 3** — the Director orders the first Blacksite level
re-sealed with crews inside it, and every Halvard member must decide
whether that order is one they carry out.

### Relays

The Spire has no relays; Halvard cares about the **Scour outer-ring
relays** (its patrols stage there) and the **Undercity outworks relay**,
which sits nearest the shaft.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, Sablier:** the concession is still a Halvard contract, the
  vats are still on Halvard power, and Halvard needs the Wake program's
  labour. The alliance is real and neither side enjoys it.
- **Allied, Kestrel:** Kestrel is a Halvard subsidiary on paper and an
  independent power in fact. Halvard maintains the fiction because
  Kestrel controls the wall gates.
- **Hostile, the Choir:** the Choir wants to open what Halvard sealed and
  talks to what Halvard fears. Halvard has offered bounties on their
  implants. There is no version of this that ends in neutrality.
- **Hostile, Red Sable:** Sable runs the black clinics that install
  what Halvard wants removed and sells Spire gear it should not have.
- **Hostile, the Unmoored:** the Unmoored have stolen more Site Zero
  fragments than anyone, publish what they steal, and were founded by a
  runner who went down the shaft the Lattice way. Halvard considers
  them the breach's first symptom.
- **Hostile, the Ferrymen:** Ring-fall is Halvard's property under the
  original contract and the Ferrymen have been stealing it for forty
  years. The Ferrymen consider this hilarious. Halvard does not.

## 2. Sablier Biomedical

*"We keep you."*

**Colours:** white `#F1F5F9` (256: 255, 16: 15) on cyan `#22D3EE` (256:
45, 16: 6). **Sigil:** `⌛` (ASCII `%`; "sablier" is an hourglass).
**Visual tell:** white coats, cyan cuffs, a scent of warm plastic.
Sablier field staff are the only people in the Districts who look
rested.

### History since the Severance

Before the Severance, Sablier was the occupational-health contractor
that discovered its real business was keeping Site Zero's specialists
alive, which in practice meant keeping copies of them. On the night of
the Severance Sablier had eleven thousand templates on file and no
instructions. It did the obvious thing: it started selling insurance.

By Year 5 a Sablier clone policy was the difference between a death and
an inconvenience for anyone who could afford it, and by Year 12 the
Civic Authority was buying policies for Wardens in bulk. Sablier never
had to seize power. It had everyone's body on file.

The company's relationship with the Choir is its most interesting
secret: Sablier makes the resonance implants, knows exactly what they
do, and has never said so. Halvard's bounty offer in Year 24 was
declined in a two-line letter that has since been framed in the Vatside
clinic hall.

The Wake program (Year 38) is Sablier's answer to a city that is running
out of specialists: decant the old templates, wipe them, lease them.
Legally a Wake is Sablier property for ninety days. The company calls
this "the onboarding period" and Dr. Vantongeren, who runs it, calls it
what it is in private and defends it in public.

Sablier does not want to open the Blacksite or seal it. It wants to know
what was down there that needed so many copies of so many people.

### Structure and ranks

Clinical hierarchy. Ranks by standing: **Orderly** (+30), **Technician**
(+50), **Clinician** (+70), **Attending** (+90). Attendings may use the
Sablier vat network anywhere in the city, not only at halls.

### Leadership

- **Dr. Corvin Adjei**, Chief Medical Officer (leader). Calm, kind,
  never in a hurry, has personally signed every Wake lease since the
  program began and remembers the names. He believes Sablier is the only
  faction in Karst doing an unambiguous good and is genuinely puzzled
  when people disagree. *"You died. You are here. Which part of that
  would you like me to apologise for?"*
- **Dr. Ilse Vantongeren**, director of the Wake program. See the
  bible. She is the first face every player sees and the faction's
  conscience, which she resents. *"Ninety days. Don't spend them
  finding out what I'm for."*
- **Marta Okonkwo-Reyes**, head of the template vault. Estranged sister
  of Red Sable's Tomasz Reyes, which both of them decline to discuss.
  Knows which templates are missing and has a list of who bought them.
  *"Template 4-0-1-1 was checked out in Year 19. It was not checked
  back in. Neither was the person who checked it out."*

**Hall:** `vatside-clinics`, the block adjoining the Wake Hall.
Sablier's vat is the city's default vat.

### Recruiter's pitch (Dr. Vantongeren, who does her own recruiting)

> I'm not supposed to do this part, but the recruiters they send are
> terrible. Sablier owns you for ninety days. That's the lease. After
> that you're a person, and people need a clinic that answers the door.
> We are the only faction that has never shot anyone on purpose. We are
> also the only faction that knows what you're made of, and I'll be
> honest, that's the part that should interest you. Work for us and I'll
> show you your template file. Take the card. Or don't; I'll still fix
> you.

### Vendor

Medical consumables at the best prices in the city, implants (every
legal class), surgery at list price with the lowest failure rate,
detox, drug **counters** (items that end a substance's effect early),
and resonance implants at Technician rank and above to Cantors with the
required Choir standing.

### Contracts

Templates: *Retrieval* (recover a dead client's secured slot items from
a corpse cache before the timer), *Escort* (walk a clinician to a Sink
black clinic and back), *Audit* (hack a black clinic's core and lift
its implant ledger).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — a Wake decanted the same day as the player
has walked out of Vatside with a template that was never supposed to be
in the vault, and Vantongeren wants them found before Halvard does.
**Season 2** — Okonkwo-Reyes's list of missing templates points at a
buyer in the Silt. **Season 3** — Adjei is offered, by someone below,
the complete Site Zero template archive, and asks a crew to go and see
what "complete" means.

### Relays

Sablier cares about the **Vatside relay** (its own district) and,
quietly, the **Sink cable-car relay**, which controls how easily its
clinicians reach the black clinics it pretends not to supply.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, Halvard:** the concession, the power, the labour. Sablier
  keeps the alliance because Halvard could turn the vats off, and
  Halvard keeps it because Sablier could turn the copies off.
- **Hostile, the Unmoored:** the Unmoored broke into the template vault
  in Year 19, published the index, and have been publishing Wake lease
  terms ever since. Sablier does not forgive people who read its files.
  The Unmoored, for their part, consider the Wake program slavery with a
  warranty. Both are right about the other.
- **Neutral with everyone else**, including the Choir, whose implants
  it builds and whose theology it does not discuss.

## 3. Kestrel Logistics

*"Everything moves."*

**Colours:** orange `#F97316` (256: 208, 16: 11) on black `#111111`
(256: 233, 16: 0). **Sigil:** `⇶` (ASCII `>`). **Visual tell:** high-
visibility orange over black, a radio on every shoulder, boots that have
walked the Scour. Kestrel people talk in route numbers.

### History since the Severance

Kestrel was the Halvard subsidiary that ran the trams and the supply
depots, which on the day of the Severance meant it was the only
organisation in Karst that knew where the food was. Within a week it was
rationing. Within a month its ration scrip was changing hands as money.
Chits are still, technically, Kestrel promissory notes. The company has
never had to say the word "power" aloud.

The wall was Kestrel's project, built with Halvard's concrete and
Kestrel's labour. So were the gates. Kestrel decides what leaves the city
and what comes back in, and its convoys are the only lawful traffic in
the Scour. The Ferrymen are the unlawful traffic, and the two have been
at war, in the way a railway is at war with the weather, since Year 3.

The Dispatcher appeared on convoy channels in Year 9. Nobody has met
the Dispatcher. Its route calls are always right. Kestrel's official
position is that the Dispatcher is a rotating duty officer, which every
convoy driver in Karst knows to be false, and the company's willingness
to keep lying about it is the most Kestrel thing about Kestrel.

Petra Lindqvist runs operations in the Dispatcher's name. She has run
the yards for fourteen years and is the only faction leader in Karst
who takes the tram to work.

### Structure and ranks

A logistics company with a militia. Ranks by standing: **Loader** (+30),
**Driver** (+50), **Yard Chief** (+70), **Route Master** (+90). Route
Masters may call a convoy (post the Convoy event's contract early once
per season).

### Leadership

- **Petra Lindqvist**, Director of Operations (leader). Fifties, broad,
  permanently hoarse from the yards, keeps a paper ledger she does not
  trust anyone else to read. She believes Karst survives because things
  move, and that every other faction is a kind of blockage. *"I don't
  need to know what's under the city. I need to know it's not on my
  line."*
- **The Dispatcher**, a voice. See the bible. Speaks only on convoy
  channels and, occasionally, to a player it has decided is useful.
  Never uses a name. *"Route Nine, Ring-fall on the outer arc in four
  minutes. Reroute. You're welcome."*
- **Halim Osei**, Wall Marshal, commands the gate militia. Ex-Ferryman,
  which the Ferrymen have not forgotten. Dry, patient, has personally
  turned back a Ferry at the gate and lived. *"The gate opens at six.
  Not at five-fifty-nine. Ask me why once."*
- **Sunny Achterberg**, Recruiter and yard foreman. Loud, cheerful,
  missing two fingers on the left hand from a coupling, uses them to
  count off recruits.

**Hall:** `tramyard-depots`. Kestrel's vat is a Sablier vat under
contract, in the dispatch hall's basement.

### Recruiter's pitch (Sunny Achterberg)

> Everything you eat came through my yard. Everything you'll ever buy
> came through the wall on my convoy. You want to know who runs this
> city? Not the ones with the view, not the ones with the badges. The
> ones with the timetable. Kestrel pays on time, in chits, which we
> print, so we're never short. You'll walk the Scour with a rifle and a
> radio and you'll come back, because the Dispatcher doesn't lose
> convoys. Don't ask about the Dispatcher. Take the card.

### Vendor

Ammunition of every type at the best prices in the city, Scour survival
gear, carrier drones, convoy-grade vehicles as *cosmetic* items only,
banking (the Kestrel branch counter is in the hall), and the market
board's physical office.

### Contracts

Templates: *Convoy escort* (walk with an NPC convoy across a Scour ring;
rewards scale with what arrives intact), *Gate audit* (survey a set of
Tramyard cells for unlicensed traffic), *Impound* (fetch an item from
an evicted apartment's impound before the tenant reclaims it, which is
legal and feels terrible).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — a convoy comes back from the outer ring one
container heavier than it left, and Lindqvist wants it opened somewhere
the Dispatcher cannot hear. **Season 2** — the Dispatcher asks a crew,
directly, to carry something down the Undercity that it will not
describe. **Season 3** — the Dispatcher stops speaking for a week, and
Kestrel discovers it cannot route convoys without it.

### Relays

The **Tramyard relay**, the **wall-gate relays** (both), and every
**Scour route relay** its convoys pass. Kestrel is the faction most
directly hurt by losing relays.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, Halvard:** the parent company. Kestrel keeps the alliance
  because Halvard's concrete built the wall and Halvard's patrols cover
  the outer rings.
- **Allied, the Wardens:** the trams are how the Wardens move, and the
  Civic Authority's rations are Kestrel's rations. This is the most
  functional alliance in the city.
- **Hostile, Red Sable:** Sable runs everything that moves through the
  city *without* a Kestrel manifest. Every black-market chit is a chit
  Kestrel did not tax.
- **Hostile, the Ferrymen:** the Ferrymen move salvage through the
  Scour without permission and raid convoys when the ring is thin.
  Kestrel calls this banditry. The Ferrymen call it the road. This is
  the one hostility in the matrix where neither side would take a peace
  if offered, because both are making money.

## 4. Karst Civic Authority (the Wardens)

*"The Core holds."*

**Colours:** blue `#2563EB` (256: 27, 16: 4) on white `#F8FAFC` (256:
255, 16: 15). **Sigil:** `⛨` (ASCII `+`). **Visual tell:** blue coats
with white piping, a lamp on the belt, badges that are real brass
because the Authority found a stock of it in Year 2 and has never
replaced the design.

### History since the Severance

The Civic Authority existed before the Severance as the contractor
town's municipal office: permits, trams, schools. On the night of the
Severance it had forty-one police officers. It has spent forty-one years
becoming a government by doing the boring things nobody else would: the
lights, the water treatment, the schools, the tram schedule (with
Kestrel), and the Wardens.

The Wardens are not a large force. Their strength is that the Core is
small, dense, and lit, and that every Warden has a Sablier policy. A
Warden who dies in the Core is back on the plaza in twenty seconds,
annoyed. Attackers learn this once.

Marshal Odile Brann took the Wardens in Year 22 after the Sink Riot,
which the Authority's history calls a "civil disturbance" and everyone
in the Sink calls the night the trams stopped. Brann's answer to the
riot was the Curfew: seal the Core, let the Districts settle their own
scores, reopen at dawn. It worked. It has worked ever since. Nobody
outside the Core has forgiven it, and Brann has not asked them to.

The Authority's position on the Blacksite is that it is not in the Core
and is therefore not the Authority's problem, which is a lie Brann tells
because the alternative is admitting the Wardens could not hold the Core
against whatever is coming up the shaft.

### Structure and ranks

Civil police. Ranks by standing: **Auxiliary** (+30), **Constable**
(+50), **Sergeant** (+70), **Inspector** (+90). Inspectors see Marked
players on the sitrep city-wide.

### Leadership

- **Marshal Odile Brann** (leader). See the bible. Sixty, lean, walks
  the Core plaza at the same hour every night without escort, and has
  been shot on it three times. *"I have kept this plaza safe for
  nineteen years. Tell me which of those years you'd have preferred I
  hadn't."*
- **Inspector Dov Marchetti**, Precinct commander. Brann's successor if
  she ever lets there be one, methodical, decent, privately keeps a file
  on every Curfew death. *"Log it. Every one. Someday somebody's going
  to want the count and I'm going to have it."*
- **Sergeant Ama Quaye**, Recruiter and Wake Hall liaison. Young for
  the rank, was a Wake herself six years ago, treats recruits as
  colleagues. *"I did the ninety days. It's not a metaphor; it's a
  number. Make it to five, then we'll talk."*
- **Clerk Wendel Hass**, the Authority's actual administrator, a
  civilian who has run the lights and the water for thirty years and
  whom Brann obeys on anything that is not a gun. *"The pumps don't care
  who's Marshal. Neither do I."*

**Hall:** `core-precinct`, the Warden precinct on the Core plaza. The
precinct is a safe pocket even during Curfew. Its vat is a Sablier vat
under a Civic contract.

### Recruiter's pitch (Sergeant Ama Quaye)

> Look around. The lights are on. The plaza is clean. Nobody in the Core
> has been shot for something they didn't choose in nineteen years.
> That's not luck and it's not the Spire; it's us, and it's a lot less
> glamorous than it sounds. We do the Districts too, when we can. We
> don't do the Blacksite. Wardens get a Sablier policy, a lamp, a badge
> that's real brass, and the only pension in Karst. Make it to your
> ninety days first. Take the card, come find me.

### Vendor

Light armour (Warden coats), non-lethal weapons (stun batons, shock
rounds, which count as kinetic with a shock multiplier), lamps and
optics, Curfew passes (an item that lets a member enter the Core during
Curfew), and legal-only implants.

### Contracts

Templates: *Patrol* (survey a set of Terraces or Sodium Row tiles and
report), *Serve* (escort a named NPC witness from a District to the
precinct), *Marked* (kill or bring in a currently Marked player, posted
automatically when a player is Marked).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — Marchetti's Curfew file is stolen from the
precinct core and the thief is in the Sink. **Season 2** — the Authority
discovers the water treatment plant's core has been talking to something
in the Silt for a year, and Hass wants to know what it said. **Season
3** — Brann is told the shaft is opening under the Core's own
foundations and must decide whether Curfew means keeping people in.

### Relays

The Core has no relays. The Wardens care about the **Terraces relay**
and the **Sodium Row relay**, the two places where the Authority still
pretends to have jurisdiction.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, Kestrel:** the trams, the rations, the water. The Authority
  cannot function without Kestrel and Kestrel needs somebody to be the
  government so that it does not have to.
- **Hostile, Red Sable:** the Sink Riot began in Sable territory and
  Sable has been the Authority's nominated enemy ever since. Sable
  enforces its own order in Sodium Row, which is the Authority's
  jurisdiction on paper, and that insult is renewed nightly.
- **Hostile, the Unmoored:** the Unmoored have hacked the precinct
  core, the tram core, and the water core, and publish Warden rosters.
  The Wardens regard them as the reason nothing in the Core can be
  trusted to stay locked.
- **Neutral, Halvard:** the Authority keeps Halvard at arm's length on
  principle. The Spire has its own police. The Core has the Wardens. The
  two forces have never fired on each other and have never helped each
  other either.

## 5. The Ninefold Choir

*"Listen."*

**Colours:** violet `#7C3AED` (256: 93, 16: 5) on gold `#EAB308` (256:
220, 16: 11). **Sigil:** `⁂` (ASCII `*`). **Visual tell:** grey
pilgrim's cloth with a violet cord, a gold implant contact visible at
the temple, and an unnerving stillness. The Choir does not fidget.

### History since the Severance

The Choir formed in Year 21 around nine runners who had each,
independently, described the same thing: a pattern in the noise floor of
the Lattice that resolved into something like attention. They compared
notes in an Old Works switchhouse and, being runners, tried to reproduce
it. They could. Anyone with a cheap neural implant could, if they knew
how to listen and did not mind what it did to their sleep.

The order grew fast in the Long Quiet's exhausted middle years. It moved
into Chapel Ward, where the old cooling towers had the acoustics its
founders wanted, and Mother Serafine Quell, the youngest of the nine,
gave it a theology: the thing in the Blacksite is not the Custodian but
a tenant, something that grew in a house built for someone else, and it
is lonely, and loneliness is a thing people can fix.

Halvard's bounty on resonance implants in Year 24 made the Choir a
persecuted church, which was the best thing that ever happened to it.
Sablier's refusal to collect the bounty made it a supplied one.

The Choir builds implants, trains Cantors, keeps the best library of
Site Zero fragments outside the Spire (much of it stolen for them by
the Unmoored), and sends pilgrims into the Silt, some of whom come back.
It is not a suicide cult. It is a listening cult, and it is very good at
it. The Exhale, to the Choir, is not a breach. It is a reply.

### Structure and ranks

A religious order. Ranks by standing: **Novice** (+30), **Cantor of the
Ward** (+50), **Precentor** (+70), **Ninth** (+90; there are never more
than nine Ninths, and the title rotates). Ninths may hear the Tenant's
voice line during a Choir Surge.

### Leadership

- **Mother Serafine Quell**, Hierophant (leader). See the bible. Forty-
  three, small, smiles a great deal, has been listening for twenty years
  and can no longer entirely stop. She wants everyone to hear it and is
  patient about it. *"You think I'm going to tell you it loves you.
  I'm not. I'm going to tell you it *noticed* you, and that that's
  rarer."*
- **Precentor Ivo Marash**, master of implants. A former Sablier
  clinician who left with his tools and his conscience and is not sure
  which weighed more. Runs the Choir's clinic and its dissonance
  training. *"Sablier will tell you a resonance implant is a neural
  amplifier. It is. They will not tell you what it amplifies."*
- **Sister Nadezhda Iloh**, Recruiter and keeper of the fragment
  library. Was a Wake; heard the Tenant on her third day, before anyone
  had told her it was there. *"I didn't need the implant. Neither will
  you, maybe. Most people do."*
- **Brother Teodor**, the Choir's pilgrim-master, who has been into the
  Silt eleven times and speaks about it only in the present tense.
  *"It's quiet down there in a way that has a shape."*

**Hall:** `chapel-towers`, the largest of the repurposed
towers. The Choir's vat is a Sablier vat, gifted, in the crypt.

### Recruiter's pitch (Sister Nadezhda Iloh)

> Everyone else will tell you what's under the city is a hazard, or a
> secret, or a property claim. We think it's a person. Not a good one,
> not a bad one, a lonely one, and we've spent twenty years learning to
> listen to it. If you were decanted from a template Sablier won't
> discuss, you already know what I mean; you heard something in the
> vat. If you didn't, we can still teach you. The Choir asks nothing but
> attention. It gives back more than you'd think. Take the card.

### Vendor

Resonance implants (Cantors with the required standing), dissonance and
hymn program analogues (the Choir's "cantillations", which are program
items for the rig), stealth programs, Listening aids, and pilgrim's
gear: lamps, rations, and the only vendor-sold **Silt beacon** (marks
a descent cell for a crew to find later).

### Contracts

Templates: *Listen* (survey hidden cells in a sector; found cells are
reported to the library), *Retrieve* (fetch a fragment from a corporate
core, which is always Halvard's), *Pilgrim* (escort a novice NPC into
the Silt and bring them back, or bring back what they heard).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — a novice comes back from the Silt speaking
in Ninety-Nine's cadence and Quell wants to know whether that is
contact or contamination. **Season 2** — the Choir hears the Tenant ask
for something specific for the first time, and it is a person's name.
**Season 3** — a Ninth proposes going down the shaft in meatspace, not
through the Lattice, and Quell must decide whether the Choir is a church
or an expedition.

### Relays

The **Chapel Ward relay** and, above all, the **Undercity descent
relay**, which holds the nearest terminal to a stable Silt entrance. The
Choir will hold that relay against anyone.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, the Unmoored:** the Unmoored steal the fragments and the
  Choir reads them. The Unmoored think the Choir's theology is
  ridiculous and its library is the only one that matters. This is the
  city's warmest alliance and neither side would admit it.
- **Allied, the Ferrymen:** the Ferrymen have been listening to the Ring
  for as long as the Choir has been listening to the Lattice, and the
  two orders recognised each other. Ferrymen guide pilgrims to the
  outer-ring artifacts; the Choir treats the Landing's dead.
- **Hostile, Halvard:** the bounty, the sealing, and the simple fact
  that Halvard wants the thing the Choir loves buried. The Choir has
  never fired first on Halvard and never will, which Halvard's patrols
  do not reciprocate.

## 6. Red Sable

*"Everything is for sale. Everything."*

**Colours:** red `#DC2626` (256: 160, 16: 1) on black `#0A0A0A` (256:
232, 16: 0). **Sigil:** `◆` (ASCII `$`). **Visual tell:** black with a
red line somewhere (a cuff, a stripe, a scar), good boots, and the habit
of standing where the light is not.

### History since the Severance

The Sink filled in the Long Quiet with everyone the ration system
missed, and where there is a population without rations there is a
market. Red Sable was the market. It began as a protection ring in the
Sink's upper terraces, became the Sink's de facto municipality by Year
10 (it runs the cable cars, which is the only reason anyone can leave),
and by the Sink Riot in Year 22 it was a power the Authority had to
negotiate with, which it did by Curfew.

Tomasz Reyes took the syndicate in Year 30 from a predecessor who is
described in the Sink as "retired" and in Sablier's records as "not
decanted". Reyes runs Sable from a cable car that never stops moving,
which is both a security measure and a statement about the Sink: nobody
in it stays still.

Sable's business is everything the corporations will not sell: black
clinics, drugs, unlicensed implants, Spire gear with the serials burned
off, Lattice access without a Warden trace, and Sodium Row, which Sable
keeps neutral because neutral ground is where money is spent. Sable is
the faction that keeps the peace on Sodium Row by being the thing
everyone is afraid of, and it is proud of the arrangement.

Sable does not care about the Blacksite except as a source of things to
sell. That is not true. Reyes's sister keeps the template vault, and
Reyes has been buying missing templates since Year 31, and the Sink
does not know why.

### Structure and ranks

A syndicate. Ranks by standing: **Runner** (in the street sense; +30),
**Made** (+50), **Lieutenant** (+70), **Sable's Hand** (+90). A Hand may
declare a Sodium Row bar off-limits to a named player for an hour.

### Leadership

- **Tomasz Reyes**, called Sable (leader). See the bible. Forties,
  handsome in a way that is starting to cost effort, unfailingly polite,
  has never raised his voice in a recorded conversation. Hates his
  sister and calls her every week. *"I don't sell people. I sell what
  people need. The distinction has kept me alive."*
- **Yusra Demir**, the Cable Boss, who runs the Sink's cable cars and
  therefore its borders. The most powerful person in the Sink who is not
  Reyes, and the only one who tells him so. *"The car leaves when I say.
  He's on it. Do the arithmetic."*
- **Doc Fennimore**, who runs the black clinics. Not a doctor. Better
  than most. Sixty, shaking, sober for the surgeries and not otherwise.
  *"Fifteen percent. I tell everyone fifteen percent. Nobody's ever
  asked me fifteen percent of what."*
- **Lio Vance**, Recruiter, a Sodium Row fixer who works the Wake Hall
  door and is the youngest recruiter in the city. Cheerful, dishonest,
  loyal to Reyes for reasons that would take a season to explain.

**Hall:** `sink-rim`, the upper terminus where Reyes's car
docks for exactly four minutes every hour. Sable's vat is a black vat,
Sablier-built and off the books, in the terminus basement.

### Recruiter's pitch (Lio Vance)

> Ninety days, right? Ninety days you belong to a clinic. Then what? The
> corps want you on a patrol, the badges want you on a plaza, the Choir
> wants you on your knees. Sable wants you *paid*. We run the Sink, we
> run the Row, we run the only clinics that don't ask what template you
> came off. You'll never be safe with us and you'll never be broke. Mr.
> Reyes says hello. He says that to everyone. Take the card; the first
> drink's on him.

### Vendor

Drugs (every substance), black-clinic surgery, unlicensed implants with
worse failure rates and better stats, Spire-grade gear at black prices,
trace-dampening programs, fenced goods (the vendor's stock changes
hourly), and **Row passes** that lift a Hand's bar ban.

### Contracts

Templates: *Collect* (a debtor NPC in a District; bring the chits or
the debtor), *Move* (carry an unmarked package through a contested zone
without being scanned by a Warden), *Burn* (find and burn a named
runner's rig in the Lattice).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — a Sodium Row bar's core is hacked from
inside the Sink, and Reyes wants the runner alive because it was one of
his. **Season 2** — Reyes asks a crew to buy a template from someone in
the Undercity who is not, on inspection, a person. **Season 3** — the
Sink's lowest terrace opens into the Undercity for the first time, and
Sable discovers it has a border it did not know about.

### Relays

The **Sink cable-car relay** (nobody else may hold it, in Sable's view)
and the **Sodium Row relay**, which Sable holds to keep the Row neutral.
Sable regards any other faction holding the Row relay as an act of war.

### Relations rationale

- **Hostile, Halvard:** Sable sells what Halvard wants destroyed and
  Halvard's Spire security raids the Sink twice a year. There is no
  business relationship to protect.
- **Hostile, Kestrel:** every chit that moves without a manifest is a
  chit Kestrel does not tax. Kestrel calls Sable smugglers. Sable calls
  Kestrel the biggest smuggler in the city with a badge for it.
- **Hostile, the Wardens:** the Sink Riot, Curfew, and Sodium Row. The
  Wardens raid; Sable shoots back; both sides log the count.
- **Hostile, the Unmoored:** Sable sells Lattice access and the Unmoored
  give it away. The Unmoored also once published Sable's client ledger,
  which is the kind of thing Sable remembers. The Unmoored regard Sable
  as the corporations with worse manners.
- **Neutral, Sablier:** the black clinics are Sablier-supplied and Reyes's
  sister runs the vault. This neutrality is a family arrangement, and
  it holds because both siblings would rather die than discuss it.

## 7. The Unmoored

*"No wall holds a signal."*

**Colours:** green `#22C55E` (256: 41, 16: 2) on grey `#9CA3AF` (256:
247, 16: 8). **Sigil:** `⌁` (ASCII `~`). **Visual tell:** whatever they
were wearing when they sat down at the rig; a green LED somewhere; the
posture of someone who has spent more time in the Lattice than out of it.

### History since the Severance

The Unmoored are the people who found out first that the Lattice did not
stop where the maps said. In the Long Quiet the first generation of
runners, mostly Old Works kids with stolen rigs, kept finding cells that
should not have been there: descent cells, hidden gates, and, at the
bottom, the Silt. They called themselves the Unmoored because the city
had cut its cables and they thought that meant the city was adrift, and
that adrift was better than tied.

Ninety-Nine was one of them. Ninety-Nine went into the Silt in Year 17
and came back as a recording, and the recording is still the Unmoored's
founder, adviser, mascot, and most-consulted oracle. The collective keeps
it in a dead drop in Old Works and moves it when Halvard gets close.

The Unmoored have no leader on principle and a Quorum in practice: a
rotating set of runners who decide what gets published. They have
published the Sablier template index (Year 19), the Warden rosters
(annually), Sable's client ledger (Year 28, which cost them three
runners), and more Site Zero fragments than anyone else. They give
Lattice access away in the Districts, which is why every faction that
sells it hates them.

The Unmoored want the Blacksite opened. Not sealed, not listened to,
not sold: opened, and everything in it published. They believe the
Severance was the outside world cutting Karst off from something, and
that the something was information.

### Structure and ranks

A collective. Ranks by standing: **Unmoored** (+30; the collective uses
its own name as its entry rank on purpose), **Publisher** (+50),
**Quorum** (+70; may vote on what the collective publishes), **Deep**
(+90; has been below the descent cells and is trusted with Ninety-Nine's
location).

### Leadership

- **The Quorum** (leadership by vote). The collective has no leader and
  players who ask are told so. In practice three voices carry:
- **Cass Ferreira**, the Quorum's current speaker. Thirties, tired,
  chain-drinks Kestrel ration coffee, has been speaker four years
  because nobody else will. Believes in the collective more than the
  collective does. *"We don't have a leader. We have me, until someone
  better shows up. Nobody's shown up."*
- **Ninety-Nine**, the residual. See the bible. Speaks from the dead
  drop through any terminal a Deep member connects it to. Funny, warm,
  and increasingly precise about the fact that it is a recording of a
  person and not the person. *"I remember going down. I don't remember
  coming up. I think that's because I didn't."*
- **Tamsin Achebe-Roux**, Recruiter and the collective's Wake Hall
  contact, who works the door in a Sablier orderly's coat she did not
  come by honestly. *"I'll tell you what's in your lease. Free. That's
  the whole pitch, really."*
- **Orin Vasko**, the collective's best living runner, who has been into
  the Silt more times than anyone but Brother Teodor and disagrees with
  him about everything down there. *"The Choir hears a voice. I hear a
  filesystem. We're both right and only one of us can read it."*

**Hall:** `oldworks-main`, the original Lattice switching
station. The Unmoored have no vat of their own; members use Sablier's
at full price, which the collective considers a badge.

### Recruiter's pitch (Tamsin Achebe-Roux)

> Here's your lease. I pulled it this morning. Clause four says Sablier
> can re-decant you from backup if you're "materially damaged", and
> clause nine defines that. Read it later. We're the Unmoored. We don't
> have a hall with a view or a vat or a pension. We have the Lattice, all
> of it, including the parts they say aren't there, and we publish what
> we find. Ninety-Nine says hello, and Ninety-Nine's been dead twenty-
> four years, so that's something. Take the card. It's a real card;
> we're not made of chits.

### Vendor

Programs of every class, rigs and rig implants at cost, Silt maps
(partial, updated by the collective's runs), Lattice keys for gated
cells, and, at Deep rank, **residual fragments** (single-use program
items that carry a piece of a past runner's skill).

### Contracts

Templates: *Publish* (lift a specific data object from a corporate or
civic core and deliver it to the switchhouse), *Unlock* (open a gated
cell in a sector for the collective's map), *Extract* (find a runner
NPC who has been traced and jack them out before the Wardens reach their
body).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — Ninety-Nine asks to be moved somewhere it
has never been, which is a request it has never made. **Season 2** —
the collective's Silt map shows a cell that is *above* the Silt, in the
Spire's sector, and the Quorum votes to send a crew. **Season 3** — a
crew finds a second residual in the Silt that claims to be Ninety-Nine's
original, and the Quorum must decide which one to publish.

### Relays

The **Old Works relay** and the **Undercity descent relay**, which it
shares an interest in with the Choir and mostly lets the Choir hold.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, the Choir:** the Choir reads what the Unmoored steal and
  keeps it safe. The Unmoored think the Choir's theology is nonsense
  and its librarians are the only competent archivists in the city.
- **Hostile, Halvard:** the fragments, Ninety-Nine, the shaft. Halvard
  regards the Unmoored as the breach and the Unmoored regard Halvard as
  the wall, and both are correct.
- **Hostile, Sablier:** the template vault break-in and the Wake lease
  publications. The Unmoored consider the Wake program slavery with a
  warranty; Sablier considers the Unmoored the only people in Karst who
  have ever hurt it.
- **Hostile, the Wardens:** the rosters, the precinct core, the water
  core. The Wardens want the Core's locks to hold; the Unmoored do not
  believe in locks.
- **Hostile, Red Sable:** Sable sells what the Unmoored give away, and
  the collective published Sable's ledger. Three Unmoored runners were
  burned for that and the collective has not forgotten.

## 8. The Ferrymen

*"The road is the law."*

**Colours:** rust `#B45309` (256: 130, 16: 3) on bone `#E7E5E4` (256:
254, 16: 7). **Sigil:** `⛵` (ASCII `^`). **Visual tell:** layered
salvage cloth, a Ring-fall shard worn somewhere visible, wind-cut faces,
and a way of looking up at the sky that people inside the wall have lost.

### History since the Severance

Not everyone came inside when the wall went up. Some had been outside
the contractor town on the night of the Severance and could not get
back; some did not want to; some were turned away at the gates by a
Kestrel militia that had not yet learned to be polite about it. They
became the people who lived in the Scour, and the Scour is Ring-fall
country, and Ring-fall is the only thing in Karst that comes from
outside.

The Ferrymen learned the Scour the way sailors learn a sea: by routes,
by weather, by the dead. The name comes from the first clans' trade,
which was carrying salvage to the wall and people from it. "Ferry" is
the title of the clan-mother who holds the routes. There have been
eleven. Anouk is the eleventh and has held the Landing for nine years.

The Ferrymen and Kestrel have been at war since Year 3, when Kestrel
decided salvage was contraband, and the Ferrymen decided the wall was a
customer. Ferrymen raid convoys when the ring is thin and guide them
when it is not, sometimes in the same week. Halvard's patrols shoot
Ferrymen on sight for stealing what the original contract says is
Halvard's; the Ferrymen have been building their homes out of Halvard's
property for forty years and find the claim delightful.

The Ferrymen listen to the Ring. They have charts of what falls where,
and their charts are better than the Spire's, and they have noticed that
the falls are not random. The Choir and the Ferrymen recognised each
other in Year 26 and have been trading pilgrims for guides ever since.

### Structure and ranks

Clans under a Ferry. Ranks by standing: **Walker** (+30), **Roadwise**
(+50), **Charter** (+70; may read the Landing's Ring-fall chart, which
shows the next Ring-fall event's ring an hour early), **Kin** (+90).

### Leadership

- **Anouk, the Ferry** (leader). See the bible. Age unknown, weathered,
  slow to speak, holds the Landing from a chair made of a satellite's
  solar array. She does not negotiate with Kestrel; she sets prices.
  *"Inside, they think the sky is a wall. Out here we know it's a
  calendar."*
- **Rook Adeyemi**, Charter of the outer routes, the Ferrymen's best
  guide and the only person who has walked the outermost ring and come
  back three times. *"Fourth ring, you don't walk it. You wait for it to
  let you."*
- **Sel Marrow**, the Landing's salvage-mistress, who grades every
  piece that comes in and has the only intact-grade eye in the city.
  Trades with Sable and denies it. *"Hull, optics, power, compute. And
  the fifth grade, which you don't get to touch until I say."*
- **Jory Kettle**, Recruiter, who comes inside the wall on a Kestrel
  pass he should not have and works the Wake Hall door in a coat that
  smells of the Scour. Young, restless, honest.

**Hall:** `scour-landing`, the Ferrymen's settlement at the first ring,
built against the outside of the wall. The Landing has a Sablier vat,
bought outright in Year 30 with intact salvage, and Sablier services it
by convoy, which the Ferrymen guard.

### Recruiter's pitch (Jory Kettle)

> You've never seen the sky. I don't mean the Ring, everybody's seen the
> Ring through a gap in the towers. I mean *seen* it, all of it, from
> the first ring at night when a fall's coming down. Everything in this
> city that's worth having came from up there and we're the ones who go
> and get it. Kestrel calls us bandits. Halvard shoots us. The Choir
> sends us their pilgrims. The Ferry pays in salvage and salvage is
> worth more than chits. Take the card, and bring boots.

### Vendor

Salvage of every grade (the only vendor that sells intact salvage),
schematics for fabrication (Ferrymen have more than anyone), Ring-fall
weapons (fabricated, high variance), Scour survival gear, guide
contracts (an NPC guide who walks a crew across a ring), and Ring-fall
charts at Charter rank.

### Contracts

Templates: *Haul* (fetch salvage from a Ring-fall site before the
event ends; reward scales with grade), *Guide* (escort a pilgrim or a
trader NPC across a ring), *Raid* (intercept a Kestrel convoy's
container; posted automatically during Convoy events).

Story hooks: **Season 1** — an intact-grade object comes down in the
fourth ring and does not stop transmitting, and Anouk wants it brought
in before Halvard's patrol finds it. **Season 2** — Sel Marrow's charts
show the falls converging on a point, and the point is the Spire.
**Season 3** — the Ferry asks a crew to carry a piece of the Ring into
the Undercity and put it back where the Custodian can reach it, because
Anouk has decided which way the Descent should go.

### Relays

Every **Scour ring relay**; the Ferrymen regard the Scour's relays as
theirs by right and everyone else's holdings as temporary.

### Relations rationale

- **Allied, the Choir:** the Ferrymen listen to the Ring, the Choir to
  the Lattice, and the two orders recognised each other. The Landing's
  dead are given Choir rites; the Choir's pilgrims are given Ferrymen
  guides.
- **Hostile, Halvard:** Halvard claims Ring-fall as its property and
  shoots Ferrymen for taking it. The Ferrymen regard the claim as a
  joke and the patrols as weather.
- **Hostile, Kestrel:** the convoy war. Both sides profit; neither wants
  peace. See Kestrel's entry.

## 9. Freelance

*"Nobody's."*

**Colours:** none; Freelance characters show in the neutral text colour.
**Sigil:** none (ASCII `-` in lists).

Freelance is not a faction. It is the state of not having joined one,
and every character is Freelance until grade 5 and standing +30 with a
faction, and again after leaving one during the seven-day cooldown.

What Freelance means in play:

- You may enter any hall's public area and buy from its vendor at full
  price (no standing discount; no standing penalty either).
- You may take non-member contracts from every handler and from Vesper,
  who is the Freelance character's real handler and treats them as such.
- You cannot capture or hold a relay. You may stand at one; it will not
  count.
- You have no faction chat, no faction vat (Sablier's public vat serves
  you), no rank.
- In contested zones you are protected by the Marked rule: attacking a
  Freelance character marks the attacker. In open zones you are on your
  own like everyone else.
- The Wake recruiter card you took (or declined) still set your starting
  standings; Freelance is the default, not a penalty.

The city has a word for long-term Freelance characters above grade 10:
**stray**. It is not an insult in Sodium Row and is an insult everywhere
else.
