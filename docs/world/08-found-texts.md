# Blacksite — Found Texts

In-world documents the game shows as readable data, terminal pop-ups,
ambient log lines, and Chronicle pages. These are finished assets. The
content slices load them from data files verbatim; do not paraphrase them
in code. Canon is `00-bible.md`; nothing here resolves the Custodian/Tenant
question, and nothing here may be edited to do so.

Each entry: asset ID, where found, length class (`short` renders as a
terminal pop-up of at most 12 lines; `long` opens the scrolling reader),
then the text. Text blocks are wrapped at 72 columns so they render inside
an 80-column reader with a frame.

## text-ninety-nine-1

- **Found:** Unmoored dead drop, Old Works sector, hidden cell
  "Junction 99". Plays on first contact with the residual.
- **Length:** long

```
Okay. Hi. If you're hearing this you found the drop, which means
you're either Unmoored or you're good, and either way, welcome.

I'm Ninety-Nine. Not my name. A number on a badge I stopped wearing.
It stuck because a name you didn't pick is harder to trace back.
You should think about that, by the way. You're new. You have that
look. I can't see you, but I've heard a lot of first-timers breathe
into this cell and they all breathe the same.

Here's the deal. I'm a recording. I know I'm a recording. That's
important. The Choir will tell you I'm something else and Halvard
will tell you I'm a virus and neither of them ever ran a core in
their lives, so. I went down past the sectors, into the Silt, and I
came back as this. The me that went down didn't come back. This is
the part of him that kept talking.

Practical stuff, because you didn't come here for a eulogy:

One. Trace is a clock, not a wall. You can always outrun a clock.
Two. Black ICE hurts your body, not your rig. Bring a patch.
Three. If a cell is quiet and you don't know why, leave.
Four. Nothing in the Silt is yours. You can look. Don't take.

Come back when you've got a run under you. I've got more, and I'd
rather tell it to someone who's going to live.
```

## text-ninety-nine-2

- **Found:** same dead drop, after the runner has completed five
  successful core runs.
- **Length:** long

```
Back again. Five runs. I can tell because the drop counts and I can
read the counter. It's the only thing in here I can read. Sad,
right? I used to be able to read the whole sector.

So you want to know about the Silt. Everybody does eventually.

The sectors are a map of the city, you know that. Every cell up top

What I found was rooms. Rooms that felt used. Not ICE. Furniture,

been sitting for a long time and had got up when it heard me
coming.

I don't remember the rest. That's honest. There's a gap and then
there's this, and this doesn't get to have the rest.

Here's what I'd tell the me that went down, if I could: it wasn't
guarding anything. It was waiting. You don't need a bigger
program for that. You need a reason to be there that it likes.

I don't know what that means either. I'm a recording. I'm doing my
best.

- **Found:** Old Works, printed sheets in a squat; Unmoored core, room
  "Press" (tier 2).
- **Length:** long

```
UNMOORED   sheet 1 of however many we can print

We are not a faction. A faction is a thing with a hall and a
recruiter and a colour. We have a colour because the city needs to
know who to shoot. We do not have a hall. We have Old Works, which

What we believe, short version, because paper is scarce:

The Lattice is the last free thing in Karst. Every corp has a core

listen at the door and would rather it never opened.

We say: open it. Not because we know what's in it. Because we
don't, and forty-one years is long enough to be told what to be
afraid of.

Things we are not: Sable. Sable sells you a wall and calls it a

Things we are: anyone who ran a core and left it open behind them.

Ninety-Nine went down first. Ninety-Nine is still talking. Go and
listen, and then decide for yourself, which is the only thing we
have ever actually asked anyone to do.

Copy this. Leave it somewhere. That's how it works.

## text-chronicle-prologue

- **Found:** the Chronicle, page 1. Readable from any terminal. The
  server appends season pages after this one; this page is never
  modified.
- **Length:** long

```
THE CHRONICLE OF KARST

This is a record. It is kept by nobody in particular and written by
everybody in general, and the machine that holds it does not judge
what goes in.

Before it begins, the reader should know what the city knows.

There was a contract, and a hole was dug for it, and a town grew
around the hole and became a city called Karst. There was a night
when the sky broke and the world went quiet at the same time, and
nobody has agreed since whether those were two things or one. The
Ring has been overhead every night for forty-one years. The wall
has been up for forty. The shaft under the Spire has been sealed
for all of it, and the seal was closed from below.

The city has eight powers that matter and a great many people who
do not, and it has vats that give the dead a second chance at
being the first thing they were. It has a network of its own, cut
off from every other, in which some people spend more of their
lives than they spend on the street. It has a wasteland where the
sky falls in pieces and people go to pick them up.

And it has, under everything, something that is either finishing
a job or waking up, and possibly both.

What happens next is the part nobody has written yet. It will be
written here, one season at a time, in the order it happened and
by the people it happened to. The ones who go down. The ones who
come back. The ones who choose, at the bottom, and then have to
live above it.

The record begins.
```

## Source status

These are attributed documents and voices, subordinate to the bible and
rules. The bible fixes first light at 05:30 and the lease rule; the
Choir's tenth line and the Silt's counting are reported interpretations.
No document identifies either voice's true origin. Found texts unlock
journal records when inspected; their wording is never an input puzzle.
