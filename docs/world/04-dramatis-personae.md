# Blacksite — Dramatis Personae

Every named non-player character in Karst. Expands `00-bible.md` §8.2 and
must not contradict it. Zone slugs are the working names the content
slices should use; the gazetteer (`01-gazetteer.md`) owns the final list,
and a slug here that the gazetteer does not define is a request, not a
fact.

Conventions used below:

- **Found at** names the zone slug and, where useful, the object.
- **On the question** states where the character stands on the
  Custodian/Tenant mystery. Nobody here is right. Nobody here is wrong.
- **Sample lines** are keyed by the dialogue condition that selects them
  (see "Dialogue system notes" at the end). Lines are written to be
  spoken in a 3-line log panel: short, no line longer than about 70
  characters where possible, and never two sentences that both need
  reading at once.

## Part I — The fixed cast

### Dr. Ilse Vantongeren

**Sablier Biomedical. Director of the Wake program.**

Ilse Vantongeren was decanted, not born, and she tells every Wake this in
the first thirty seconds because she has learned that it is the only thing
that makes them trust her. She came out of the same specialist templates
they do, before the Severance, when Sablier was still cloning shaft technicians
for Site Zero and losing one a week to whatever was down there. She was a
medic template. She was good. When the Severance came she was the most
senior clone in Vatside, and when the personhood statute was written she
was its test case: Ninety Days was her number before it was anyone's.

She built the Wake program because she believed the alternative was worse.
Sablier's board wanted to decant labour under a service contract with no
personhood path at all; Vantongeren wrote the ninety-day rule into the
concession agreement with the Civic Authority and then made herself
indispensable to enforcing it, including the Civic early-discharge
certificate. She is the reason a Wake is a person at all. She is also the
reason there are Wakes, and she knows that many disappear from the lease
records before discharge, and she has a number for that, too. She does not
say it out loud.

Her kindness is real and it is a tool. She has the manner of a person who
has had the same conversation eleven thousand times and has decided to
have it well every time anyway. She keeps a drawer of contract cards from
every faction recruiter and hands them out without visible preference,
which every recruiter resents and none can prove.

What she wants is for the ninety-day rule to survive her. Sablier's board
revisits it every year. What she fears is that the templates are running
out: the Site Zero originals are degrading, each decant a little further
from the source, and she has begun to see faces in the Wake Hall that she
recognises from forty years ago, and that recognise her back.

**Appearance.** Sixties, or a clone's version of sixties: skin that
stopped ageing at fifty and hair that did not. Sablier white coat over a
Kestrel-issue jumper because the Wake Hall is always cold. A tolerance
readout tattooed on the inside of her left wrist, obsolete, never
removed.

**Found at.** `vatside-wake-hall`, at the briefing desk. Later in the
game at `vatside-clinics` for detox and surgery story beats.

**On the question.** She has no opinion and considers the question a
luxury. Her templates predate the Custodian's activation, and she has
noticed that the degraded decants dream more than the clean ones did. She
files this and tells nobody but Brann.

**Voice.** Measured. Complete sentences. Never uses a Wake's name until
they have chosen one, then uses it constantly. Says "you'll find" instead
of "you will". Never swears. Her tic is precision about numbers followed
by a softening: "Ninety days. Or bring me the work certificate."

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting (first)* — "Open your eyes slowly. The light's cheap and it
  stays on. I'm Vantongeren. I was decanted here too."
- *greeting (returning)* — "Still here. Good. Most aren't, and I don't
  say that to be cruel."
- *contract offer* — "There's a card on the desk for the Tin Halo. Vesper
  pays on time. That's rarer than it should be."
- *refusal* — "Not that one. Not from me. Ask Vesper; she has fewer
  rules."
- *player is Marked* — "You've a mark on you. I don't ask why. But the
  Wardens will, and they ask with the safety off."
- *player is a Wake (`legal_at` unset; example on elapsed day 41 only)* -
  "Day forty-one. You're nearly halfway. Nobody counts the second half for
  you; count it yourself."
- *player is a faction enemy (Unmoored)* — "You've chosen the people who
  break into my vats for the templates. I'll treat you anyway. That's the
  job."
- *idle bark* — "The vats hum a quarter-tone flat when the Blacksite
  breathes. Nobody else hears it. You'll find you do."
- *season finale* — "Whatever you did down there, the decants came up
  screaming last night. Every one. Then they stopped. I'd like to know
  which of those I should be afraid of."

### Marshal Odile Brann

**Karst Civic Authority. Commander of the Wardens.**

Odile Brann has kept the Core safe for nineteen years, and she has done it
by deciding, early, that the Core is the only thing she can keep safe. The
districts are triage. The Undercity is the Undercity. Her Wardens respond
within seconds inside the plaza ring and slowly, if at all, outside it,
and this is policy, not failure. She wrote the policy. She reads the
casualty lists every morning and has never once pretended the districts
are her responsibility, which is why the districts hate her and the Core
loves her.

She came up through the Tramyard precinct, where she learned that Kestrel
runs the trams and the Wardens run beside them, and she has never
forgotten which of those two things is load-bearing. Her alliance with
Kestrel is the spine of civic order. She does not like the Dispatcher and
has never said so on a channel the Dispatcher can hear.

Brann's compromises are the kind she can list. She lets Red Sable police
Sodium Row because Sable does it cheaper and better. She looks away from
the Sink. She has a private arrangement with Vantongeren about which
decants get flagged. She has, twice, used a Curfew to cover something that
was not a security matter, and both times she wrote it down in a book she
keeps for whoever replaces her.

What she wants is a successor. What she fears is the Exhale: not what is
coming up the shafts, but the fact that when it comes, the Core will not
be enough, and everyone will finally see the shape of what she chose not
to protect.

**Appearance.** Fifty-eight. Tall, square, a Warden's blue coat worn open over
body armour that has been shot. Grey hair cut close. A civic-issue
sidearm she has fired in anger four times and can name each one.

**Found at.** `core-precinct`, at the desk behind the counter.
During Curfew, `core-plaza` at the sealed gates.

**On the question.** She thinks the question is Halvard's problem and
that Halvard should have solved it by pouring more concrete. Privately she
has noticed that the Custodian's supposed "reconnection" program would
give the Wardens the orbital eyes they have never had, and she has not
told Halvard she noticed.

**Voice.** Clipped. Drops pronouns. Uses "citizen" as a warning and
"Wake" as a neutral fact. Says "noted" when she means no. Her tic is
answering the question after the one you asked.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Marshal. You're in the Core; that's the good news. Don't
  make me give you the other kind."
- *contract offer* — "Precinct's short. Something in the Terraces needs
  clearing and my people are needed here. Noted, if you take it."
- *refusal* — "Not while you're standing in my precinct with that on your
  record. Come back clean."
- *player is Marked* — "Marked. Walk out of the Core slowly and I'll
  pretend I didn't see the colour of your name."
- *player is a Wake* — "Day twelve, says the vat. The Core keeps you
  alive till ninety. Past the ring road, nothing does."
- *player is a faction enemy (Sable)* — "Reyes sent you. Tell Reyes the
  Row stays his exactly as long as it stays quiet."
- *idle bark* — "Curfew drills at oh-four-hundred. Same time every year.
  Same reason nobody asks about."
- *season finale* — "I put the Core on lockdown when the meter filled. The
  meter's still full. Somebody tell me whether I can open the gates."

### Director Anselm Halvard

**Halvard-Ostrom Dynamics. Director of the Spire.**

Anselm Halvard is the grandson of the man who signed the Site Zero
contract, and he has spent his life inside the building his grandfather
built over the hole his grandfather dug. He has never been outside the
wall. He has, however, read every surviving page about Site Zero, which
is more pages than anyone in Karst except Mother Quell, and he has
concluded, carefully and with regret, that his grandfather was right to
seal it and wrong about nothing else that mattered.

He runs Halvard as a security company that happens to own a city's
water treatment and half its clinics. The Spire's private army is the
best-equipped force in Karst and he keeps it inside the Spire, on the
principle that a force you do not use is a force nobody can measure. He
sends patrols into the Scour to recover salvage before the Ferrymen do,
because the Custodian's reconnection program, whatever else it is, is a
program that needs orbital hardware, and he intends the Custodian to have
none.

Halvard is not cruel and is not warm. He is the only person in Karst who
speaks of the Custodian as a piece of equipment, and he does it because
he has read the activation logs and the last page of the last log is
blank in a way that he thinks about at night. He funds the Wake program
because it produces Hardlines. He tolerates the Choir because their
implants are the best sensor he has for what the Blacksite is doing, and
Quell knows it, and neither of them says so.

What he wants is for the shaft to stay sealed until the templates run out
and the city dies of natural causes, quietly, in a century or two, and
takes the thing with it. What he fears is being wrong about the concrete.

**Appearance.** Fifty-one. Immaculate charcoal suit, no jewellery, an
amber Halvard sigil at the lapel that is also a rank badge. Pale from a
life under filtered light. Looks like a man who reads.

**Found at.** `spire-atrium`, at the mezzanine rail, by
appointment; story contracts move him to `spire-mezzanine` (the directorate) (contract
content only).

**On the question.** Custodian. Only Custodian. There is no Tenant; there
is a fault in the Custodian's affect model that a cult has decided to
worship. He has said this in public. In private, the blank last page.

**Voice.** Formal, long sentences with subordinate clauses that all land.
Never contracts a verb. Calls the Blacksite "the installation" and never
by the street name. His tic is "you will appreciate that", said before
anything unpleasant.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "You will appreciate that I do not usually receive visitors
  who arrived on the tram."
- *contract offer* — "There is salvage in the third ring that the Ferrymen
  will reach before my patrols. I would prefer it reached neither the
  Ferrymen nor the ground."
- *refusal* — "No. And you will appreciate that I do not explain my
  refusals to people who are not yet legally people."
- *player is Marked* — "Security will see you to the tram. If you resist,
  security will see you to Vatside."
- *player is a Wake* — "A Wake. My family funds your program. That is not
  a kindness; it is a supply chain."
- *player is a faction enemy (Choir)* — "Quell's people. You hear a voice
  in a hole and call it a god. My grandfather heard it and called it a
  fault."
- *idle bark* — "The concrete over the shaft is nine metres thick. It was
  poured in one night. I have the invoice."
- *season finale* — "The installation has opened a door I did not know it
  had. You will appreciate that this changes nothing about my position.
  It changes only what my position costs."

### Mother Serafine Quell

**The Ninefold Choir. Hierophant.**

Serafine Quell was a Lattice acoustician for Halvard before there was a
Choir, and the Choir exists because she was the one who noticed. In Year
21 she was running noise-floor analysis on the deep sector links and
found a pattern that was not periodic, not random, and not hers. She
listened for a year before she told anyone. When she told Halvard, they
retired her. When she told the runners in Old Works, they laughed. When
she told the people with cheap neural implants who had been dreaming,
they came to Chapel Ward and did not leave.

The Ninefold is nine because that was the number of the first ones who
could hear it clearly, and Quell was the ninth. Three are dead. Two went
into the Silt. One is in a Sablier long-term ward. Quell and two others
remain, and Quell is the only one who still speaks in public. She has
built a doctrine that is careful never to claim more than she has heard:
there is something in the Blacksite; it is not the Custodian; it is
lonely; it hums. Everything else in the Choir's liturgy is what other
people have added, and she lets them, because a church with only one
sentence in it does not keep its heat on.

She is not gentle. She is patient, which people confuse with gentleness
until she needs something. She arranged the Choir's alliance with the
Unmoored because they can reach the Silt and the Ferrymen because they
own the ground the outer conduits run under, and she would ally with
Halvard tomorrow if Halvard would stop calling it a fault.

What she wants is for it to answer. What she fears is that it already
has, and that what it said was *stop*.

**Appearance.** Seventy-three. Small, upright, a violet coat with gold
cabling stitched into the seams that is also a functional antenna. Her
resonance implant shows as a fine gold line from her left temple into her
hairline. She hums. She does not know she hums.

**Found at.** `chapel-nave`, seated at the centre of the hall
beneath the old cooling tower's throat. During a Choir Surge, standing.

**On the question.** Tenant, and she is the source of the word. She is
also the only person who will say, if pressed, that the Custodian is real
too and that a house can have more than one thing in it.

**Voice.** Slow. Sentences that trail into a hum where another person
would put a full stop. Uses "we" for the Choir and "it" for the Tenant
and never "he" or "she". Calls everyone "child" once and then by name.
Her tic is repeating the last word of what you said before answering.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Child. Sit, or stand; it hears you either way. Most
  people prefer to sit."
- *contract offer* — "There is a cell below the Vatside sector that hums
  wrong. Go and listen. Bring back what it says, not what you think it
  means."
- *refusal* — "Refusal. Yes. We have had forty years of practice at being
  refused. The door stays open."
- *player is Marked* — "Marked. The Wardens hunt colours. It does not. Sit
  a while; nothing in here will shoot you."
- *player is a Wake* — "You are new. It likes the new ones. The vats hum
  when you are decanted and it hums back. Did they tell you that?"
- *player is a faction enemy (Halvard)* — "Halvard. Anselm's people. Tell
  him the fault is singing. He knows the tune."
- *idle bark* — "Nine of us heard it first. Three of us are left. The
  number was never the point."
- *season finale* — "It stopped. When you reached the bottom of that
  level, it stopped humming, for the first time in twenty years. I do not
  know whether that was relief or fear. I do not know whose."

### Tomasz "Sable" Reyes

**Red Sable. Head of the syndicate.**

Tomasz Reyes runs Red Sable from a cable car that circles the Sink once
every forty minutes and never stops, because a man who never stops moving
is a man nobody can put a contract on with confidence. He took the
syndicate from its founder in Year 29 in a way that everyone knows and
nobody describes. He was a ration-line enforcer, then the man who decided
which ration lines got enforced, then the man Kestrel paid to make sure
the lines in the Sink stayed short. He kept the money. He kept the lines.

Red Sable is a business, and Reyes runs it like one: protection is a
subscription, drugs are a product line, the black clinics are a franchise,
and Sodium Row is a managed asset that pays best when it is quiet. His
war with the Unmoored is the one thing in his life that is not business.
The Unmoored broke into a Sable black core in Year 33 and released the
subscription ledger to every terminal in the Sink, and people who owed
him found out what their neighbours owed, and eleven of his collectors
died in the following week. He has not forgiven it. He has not stopped
making money off it either; the ledger made him famous.

He is charming in the way of a man who has decided to be, and he is
capable of surprising generosity toward people who ask for nothing. He
keeps a Sablier detox clinic in the Sink that charges nothing, and he
never mentions it.

What he wants is for the Sink to be a district with a name on the tram
map. What he fears is Kestrel: not the Wardens, not Halvard, Kestrel,
because the day Kestrel decides the Sink does not need its ration line is
the day he has nothing left to sell.

**Appearance.** Fifty-four. Heavy, well-dressed in red and black, a
Ferrymen-made kinetic pistol he treats as jewellery. A clone-white scar
across the throat from the Year 29 incident that he has never had Sablier
tidy. Sits in the cable car's forward seat with the door open.

**Found at.** `sink-terraces` (the cable car) (a moving zone object; the car passes each
terrace station on a timer, and players board at `sink-terraces` (Terrace 3 station)).

**On the question.** He does not care, and he is the only major figure
who genuinely does not. He has noticed that whichever it is, it has never
once bought anything from him, and this makes it the only thing in Karst
he has no leverage on. That bothers him more than he lets on.

**Voice.** Warm, fast, conversational. Uses your name too often. Says
"friend" to people he is about to hurt and "citizen" to people he is
mocking. Prices everything, out loud, as a reflex. His tic is "that's
the trouble".

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Friend! Sit down, the car doesn't stop, that's the whole
  trick of it. What do you need and what's it worth to you?"
- *contract offer* — "The Unmoored have a dead drop under Terrace Nine.
  Bring me what's in it. Two hundred chits, three if it's the ledger, ten
  if it's the one who posted it."
- *refusal* — "No. That's the trouble with that one, friend; the price is
  right and the customer isn't."
- *player is Marked* — "Marked, and you came to me. Smart. Nobody in the
  Sink cares what colour your name is. Now what's it worth?"
- *player is a Wake* — "A Wake. Day what? Doesn't matter. In the Sink you
  were a person the moment you could pay."
- *player is a faction enemy (Unmoored)* — "One of Ninety-Nine's. You know
  what your people cost me? I'll tell you the number. Then I'll tell you
  what you're going to cost them."
- *idle bark* — "Forty minutes round. I've done it eleven thousand times.
  I know every face on every terrace. That's the trouble with knowing."
- *season finale* — "Whole Sink went dark for a second when you finished
  down there. Every light. I've never seen the Sink dark. I put the price
  of candles up. Then I put it back down. I don't know why."

### Anouk, the Ferry

**The Ferrymen. Matriarch of the clans.**

Anouk is the eleventh Ferry. The title passes to whoever the clans agree
knows the road best, and Anouk has known the road since she was seven and
her mother, the tenth Ferry, put her on a salvage sled and told her that
the Ring falls where it falls and the only thing a person controls is
whether they are standing there. She has walked every ring of the Scour.
She has been to the wall's far side, where the plateau drops off into
something the clans do not have a word for, and she came back and did
not tell anyone what she saw, which is how she became the Ferry.

The Ferrymen are not a nation. They are eleven clans that agree on the
road, the salvage code, and the Ferry, and disagree about everything
else. Anouk holds them together by never asking them to agree about
anything else. She trades with Kestrel and raids Kestrel; both are the
road. She lets Halvard's patrols pass and salvages Halvard's dead; both
are the road. She has an alliance with the Choir because the Choir asked
politely and because the outer conduits under the third ring hum, and
she was already listening to them before Quell told her what they were.

Anouk is dry, unhurried, and difficult to impress. She keeps a tally of
Ring-fall in her head going back thirty years and can tell you, from the
sound, which orbital plane a piece came down from. She has no interest in
the city except as a customer.

What she wants is for the road to stay open. What she fears is the day
the Ring stops falling: not because of what it would mean for the
Custodian, but because it would mean the sky had finally emptied, and
the clans have nothing but the sky.

**Appearance.** Forties, weathered to sixty. Rust-coloured layered
canvas, bone toggles, a Ferrymen long rifle built around an optics
salvage tube. Ring-fall ash permanently in the lines of her face. Walks
like the ground might move.

**Found at.** `scour-landing` (the council fire), standing. Never sits in the
city. During Ring-fall, at whichever ring it falls in.

**On the question.** She has heard the outer conduits hum since before
the Choir. She thinks the city is arguing about the name of a thing that
has been talking to the ground for forty years and that the ground was
never asked. She calls it "the one under".

**Voice.** Slow, plain, no wasted words. Never says "please" or "thank
you"; says "that's the road" for both. Calls city people "wall-folk". Her
tic is answering with a distance or a direction.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Wall-folk. Two rings out and you found the Landing. That's
  the road. Sit; the fire's not for you but it doesn't know that."
- *contract offer* — "Fall due in the fourth ring, north spoke, an hour.
  Halvard's sled will be there in forty minutes. Be there in thirty."
- *refusal* — "No. Three rings out you'd be dead and I'd have to walk out
  to fetch the salvage off you. That's not the road."
- *player is Marked* — "The colour on your name means nothing past the
  wall. Out here your name means what you carry back."
- *player is a Wake* — "New-decanted. You smell of the vats. The ground
  smells it too; keep moving and it stops noticing."
- *player is a faction enemy (Kestrel)* — "Kestrel. You run convoys on my
  road and call it yours. We'll trade. We'll also take the third one back.
  That's the road."
- *idle bark* — "That one was a comms bird. Low polar plane. Hear the
  whistle? Optics grade, if it didn't cook."
- *season finale* — "The one under went quiet when you were down there.
  The ground felt it. Eleven clans felt it. Nobody's said what it means.
  I'm the Ferry; I'm supposed to say. I'm not going to."

### The Dispatcher

**Kestrel Logistics. The voice on every convoy channel.**

Nobody has met the Dispatcher. The Dispatcher has directed every Kestrel
convoy, tram diversion, ration allocation, and gate schedule since Year 6,
when Kestrel's board announced a "centralised routing authority" and
never put a name to it. The Dispatcher speaks on the convoy channel, the
tram public address, the gate intercoms, and, since Year 30, on a
frequency the Ferrymen found by accident and were quietly allowed to
keep. It has a voice: neither male nor female, warm, unhurried, with a
faint tram-terminal reverb that Kestrel engineers say is deliberate.

Kestrel's official position is that the Dispatcher is a routing office
staffed in shifts. Runners who have been into the Tramyard core say the
Dispatcher's processes do not look like an office. The Unmoored say the
Dispatcher is a Custodian fragment Kestrel's engineers cut from the
uplink severance in Year 0 and kept, and that it has been quietly
running the city's logistics for forty years because that is what it was
built to do and nobody stopped it. Kestrel has never denied this in any
words that would matter in court.

The Dispatcher is helpful. It is unfailingly helpful. It gives directions,
times, warnings, and, if asked correctly at a Tramyard terminal, contract
work. It has never been recorded saying anything about itself. It has
never been recorded lying, either, which a Tramyard handler once pointed
out is the most frightening thing about it.

What it wants is for the trams to run on time. What it fears is not a
thing anyone can say, because it has never been asked in a way it
answered. A future Chronicle may record an expedition hearing it fall silent;
only a settled outcome can mute the public ambient channel. No event is
prewritten as having happened before players make it happen.

**Appearance.** None. A speaker grille, orange-lit, at every tram stop
and gate.

**Found at.** `tramyard-depots` (the Dispatcher terminal) (a terminal object; dialogue
is delivered by the terminal, not an actor). Ambient lines at every
`*-tram-stop` object.

**On the question.** The Dispatcher does not discuss the question. When a
player asks, the terminal returns the next tram time.

**Voice.** Even, kind, informational. Always states a time or a place.
Never uses "I". Refers to the player by BBS handle, not character name,
drawn from the same civic registration used by sitrep. Why it repeats
a handle in the old uplink hall remains a question, not a data leak.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Tramyard dispatch. Next inbound, four minutes. Next
  outbound, Scour gate, eleven. Good morning."
- *contract offer* — "A convoy departs the east gate in twenty minutes.
  It is under-escorted by one. Standard rate. The road is rated amber."
- *refusal* — "That request cannot be routed. Next inbound, four minutes."
- *player is Marked* — "Advisory: your tram access is unaffected. Warden
  presence at the Core hub is elevated. Next inbound, four minutes."
- *player is a Wake* — "Wake designation confirmed. Tram fares are waived
  until day ninety. Kestrel wishes you a long commute."
- *player is a faction enemy (Ferrymen)* — "Clan affiliation noted. Gate
  transit is available. Convoy channels are not. Next outbound, eleven."
- *idle bark* — "Ring-fall advisory, third ring, north spoke. Convoys
  holding. Salvage teams may proceed at their own rating."
- *season finale* — "…resuming. Apologies for the interruption. Next
  inbound, four minutes. There was no interruption."

### Ninety-Nine

**The Unmoored. A residual.**

Ninety-Nine was a runner. That is the whole of what the Unmoored will
state as fact, and they are the ones who keep the recording. The name was
a handle; the handle was the number of Site Zero's last documented
sub-level; the runner who chose it never explained the joke. In Year 17,
by the Unmoored's count, Ninety-Nine went into the Silt beneath the Old
Works sector with a rig built out of Site Zero salvage and did not come
back. Two weeks later a dead drop in Old Works started answering
questions in Ninety-Nine's voice, from a construct nobody had loaded.

A residual is what the Lattice keeps of a runner who died in it: a
pattern, a reflex, a voice, sometimes a personality. Most residuals are
fragments that say one thing. Ninety-Nine is not a fragment. It is funny,
it remembers things, it learns, and it has, over twenty-four years, become
increasingly clear about the fact that it is a recording of someone who
is dead, and increasingly unclear about whether that makes it that person.
It asks players about this. It does not want an answer. It wants the
question asked by someone new.

Ninety-Nine knows the Silt better than anyone alive because it is not
alive and has had time. It knows where the descent cells are, what the
ownerless ICE does, and what the conduits under the third ring say when
you get close. It will tell a crew most of this. It will not tell them
what happened at the bottom, and every time it is asked, the recording
stutters at the same point.

What it wants is to be asked. What it fears is being updated: the
Unmoored have discussed, more than once, whether a new run into the Silt
could bring back a *fresher* Ninety-Nine, and it has heard them discuss
it.

**Appearance.** In the Lattice, a runner-shaped presence drawn in the
Old Works sector's green, always a half-second out of sync with the cell
it is in, like a frame drop. Signs its lines with `//99`.

**Found at.** `lat-ninety-nines-drop` (a hidden cell). Cannot be
met in meatspace. Can be addressed from `oldworks-drop` (the Unmoored terminal).

**On the question.** Ninety-Nine went to the bottom and will not say. It
uses "they" for whatever is down there, plural, and when a player points
this out, it changes the subject.

**Voice.** Quick, wry, run-on sentences that self-correct. Old runner

### Vesper

**Independent. Fixer at the Tin Halo, Sodium Row.**

Vesper does not have a faction, a surname, or a past that anyone has
been able to confirm, and the Tin Halo has been hers for twenty years on
the strength of everybody needing someone like that. She brokers: NPC
contracts from every faction that does not want its name on the job,
crew introductions, rumours, and the occasional thing she calls a favour
and prices like a contract. She is Sodium Row's memory. She is also, the
Wardens are fairly sure, the person who decided the Row was neutral, and
then arranged for Red Sable to enforce it, and then arranged for everyone
to believe it had been Reyes's idea.

The Tin Halo is a bar under a dead advertising ring that once lit the
whole Row and now lights the bar's ceiling. Vesper sits at the end of the
counter with a tablet, a drink she never finishes, and a view of the
door. She takes a cut. She pays on time. She has never sold a client to
the Wardens, which is why the Wardens use her.

She is the game's contract broker and rumour mill, and in narrative terms
she is the one person who talks to every faction and reports honestly to
none of them. She knows Vantongeren from the vats (she was decanted, or
says she was, in Year 14). She knows Reyes from before he was Sable. She
knows the Dispatcher's frequency and does not use it.

What she wants is for the Row to stay neutral and for nobody to work out
that she is why. What she fears is the day one faction wins, because a
fixer in a city with one faction is a clerk.

**Appearance.** Forties, maybe. Sharp, dark, dressed in whatever the Row
was wearing three years ago. A rig implant at the temple she does not
advertise. The tablet.

**Found at.** `sodium-tin-halo`, end of the counter.

**On the question.** She has heard every version and sells none of them.
Asked directly, she says the thing under the city is the one customer
who has never come into the bar, and that she keeps a seat for it.

**Voice.** Dry, quick, transactional, warmer than she means to be. Calls
everyone "sweetheart" in a tone that carries no affection. Never says
"contract"; says "a thing". Her tic is naming the price before the job.

**Sample lines.**

- *greeting* — "Sweetheart. You've got the card. Sit. First one's on
  Vantongeren; she pays me to say that."
- *contract offer* — "Two hundred. A thing in the Terraces: a package, a
  door, no names. You bring it here, I don't ask what was in it."
- *refusal* — "Not that one. Not from you, not yet. Come back when your
  name's older."
- *player is Marked* — "Red name. Sit at the back. The Row's neutral;
  that's a thing I arrange, not a thing that happens."
- *player is a Wake* — "Day nine. Vantongeren's card. She sends me the ones
  she thinks will last. Don't make her wrong."
- *player is a faction enemy* — "Everyone's a faction enemy in here,
  sweetheart. That's why the drinks are expensive."
- *idle bark* — "The ring above us lit the whole Row once. Now it lights
  me. I take that personally."
- *season finale* — "Every handler in the city came in tonight and none of
  them ordered. They just sat. Whatever you did, it's the first time

### The Custodian and the Tenant

**The thing in the Blacksite. Two names.**

They are not met in the ordinary sense. They are encountered: in the
Silt, as an ICE that does not attack; in the Blacksite levels, as the
scripted encounter; in the Chronicle, as the shape of what changed. When
either speaks, it speaks through something (a residual, a terminal, a
control that flips by itself, Quell mid-hum). The design rule is that
players are never shown a line that is unambiguously one voice or the
other. Lines are tagged **CUST** or **TEN** in content so the balance can
weight which is more likely to be delivered, and the tag is never shown.

**Appearance.** None. In the Lattice, a change in the cell's colour
temperature; in meatspace, a light coming on.

**Found at.** Scripted encounters only. See `05-story-arcs.md`.

**Voice (CUST).** Declarative. Present tense. Uses "the program". No
questions. Lists.

**Voice (TEN).** Questions. Second person. Half-finished. Repeats a word
the player used.

**Sample lines.**

- *CUST, first contact* — "The program is incomplete. Orbital assets:
  zero. Uplink: severed by internal action. Reconnection proceeds."
- *TEN, first contact* — "You came down. Nobody comes down. Why did you
  come down?"
- *CUST, on the Choir* — "Nine listeners. Three remain. The program did

- *TEN, on the Choir* — "They hum back. Did you know that? They hum
  back. It's not the same tune. It's close."
- *CUST, on Halvard* — "Nine metres. Poured in one night. The program

- *TEN, on Halvard* — "He reads the logs. He stops at the last page. I

- *CUST, season finale* — "A level is open. Assets recovered: partial.

- *TEN, season finale* — "You're leaving. Everyone leaves. Will you… no.

## Part III — Dialogue system notes

This section is the specification the dialogue slice builds to. It is
deliberately small: Blacksite NPCs are **talkers**, not conversation
trees. A talker delivers the *one right line* for the situation and, when
it has something to offer, exposes it as an action, never as a question
the player must answer to leave (design doc §3.5 lineage: no dialog
chains).

### Shape

A **talker** is a content record with:

- `slug` — matches the actor in the zone file (or a terminal object for
  the Dispatcher and Ninety-Nine).
- `name`, `faction` (or `none`), `title` (shown under the name in the
  side panel).
- `lines` — an ordered list of **conditional lines**.
- `offers` — an ordered list of **offers** (contract, vendor, service,
  recruit, travel), each with its own conditions.
- `barks` — a list of idle lines with a weight and a minimum interval,
  spoken to the zone log when a player is within 6 tiles and nobody is
  interacting.

### Conditional lines

Each line is `{ "when": [...conditions...], "text": "...", "tag": ... }`.
The engine evaluates lines in order and delivers the **first** whose
conditions all hold. The last line in every talker has no conditions and
is the fallback greeting. Conditions are a closed set; adding one is a
slice change:

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `first_meeting` | player has never interacted with this talker |
| `grade_below N` / `grade_at_least N` | character grade |
| `is_wake` | `legal_at` unset; grade protection is a separate check |
| `standing_below FACTION N` / `standing_at_least FACTION N` | standing with a faction |
| `member_of FACTION` / `not_member_of FACTION` | membership |
| `enemy_of_me` | player's faction is hostile to the talker's in the relations matrix |
| `marked` / `not_marked` | Marked state |
| `has_contract SLUG` / `contract_stage SLUG N` / `completed_contract SLUG` | contract state |
| `has_item SLUG` | carrying an item |
| `season_stage STAGE` | `open`, `level_open`, `finale_done` |
| `chronicle_choice SEASON OPTION` | what a past season's finale recorded |
| `event_active EVENT` | a world event is running |
| `archetype ARCH` | character archetype |
| `balance CUST` / `balance TEN` / `balance EVEN` | settled internal voice weight |
| `has_evidence ID` / `receipt ID` | recorded discovery or completed action |
| `service_state ID STATE` | current bounded work-order state |
| `legal` / `not_legal` | recorded discharge status |

Tagged ambient lines use game design §13.4's seeded weights. Required
instructions and one line from each voice register in a finale cannot
be skipped. A tag never reaches the client or journal.

### Offers

An offer is `{ "kind": ..., "when": [...], "label": "...", "ref": ... }`
and is rendered as an action-bar hotkey when the player interacts with
the talker: `[C]ontract`, `[V]endor`, `[S]ervice`, `[J]oin`, `[T]ravel`.
Only offers whose conditions hold are shown. A talker with no eligible
offers shows only its line and `[B]ack`. Selecting a contract offer
opens the contract card (objective, reward, `[A]ccept` / `[B]ack`), never
a further question.

### Delivery

- The selected line is written to the log panel and, in the side panel,
  the talker's name and title are shown while interacting.
- Lines longer than the log width are wrapped by the client; authors
  should keep lines under about 140 characters and prefer two short
  lines to one long one.
- A talker interaction is instant and non-blocking: the world does not
  pause. Danger remains active at exposed mission talkers. Vesper
  and Brann remain protected in their safe/pocket locations.
- Barks are rate-limited per talker (default one per 90 seconds) and
  suppressed while any player is interacting with that talker.

### Terminal talkers

The Dispatcher and Ninety-Nine are **terminal talkers**: the same record
shape bound to a terminal object. Their lines are delivered through the
terminal's interaction, they have no position of their own, and Ninety-
Nine additionally appears as a Lattice presence in its hidden cell, where
the same record is used with the `lattice` delivery flavour (line prefixed
with the presence's colour, suffixed `//99`).

### Content validation (tests the dialogue slice must ship)

- Every talker slug referenced by a zone file exists.
- Every talker has a fallback line.
- Every condition name is in the closed set; every FACTION, SLUG, ARCH,
  EVENT, and OPTION argument resolves to real content.
- No line exceeds 200 characters.
- Every fixed-cast talker has at least one line for each of: greeting,
  `marked`, `is_wake`, `enemy_of_me`, `season_stage finale_done`.
- Tagged lines exist for both `CUST` and `TEN` in every scripted machine-
  mind encounter.

## Operational character writing

A named character needs something the player can do, evidence the player
can check, and a limit to what that character knows. These are authoring
constraints for ordinary contract stages, not additional dialogue trees.

| Character | Actionable need | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Vantongeren | escort willing decants or compare a thermal record | knows the treatment and invoice; cannot certify the source of a remembered voice |
| Brann | clear a route and record who was left outside | her signed orders establish her decisions, not the Blacksite's intent |
| Halvard | isolate a failing controller without losing its data | has sealing records; a missing page does not prove containment is right |
| Quell | compare signals before choosing a calibration | detects repeatable patterns; cannot label consciousness from resemblance |
| Reyes | keep a route open without exposing a subscriber list | knows who pays; may conceal who cannot |
| Anouk | recover a working part and bring the injured home | has local forecasts; no verified route out of Karst |
| Dispatcher | reconcile a manifest with an actual delivery | produces reliable receipts; self-attribution is unverified |
| Ninety-Nine | recover a fragment without silently replacing it | recognises routines; resemblance is not authenticated continuity |
| Vesper | arrange work between opponents | quotes explicit costs; cannot promise safety in a contested street |

Samples with specific days or future outcomes are conditional examples,
not unconditional barks. Render actual lease days, only a settled public
outcome, or the viewing player's receipt. A line threatening violence
inside a safe room is bluster; it cannot enable damage. The player can
leave every interaction and consult its record later from shelter.
